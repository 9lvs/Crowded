# Campus creators and ambassadors

A small team of students who make content and report the line on pilot nights. Works with
[SOCIAL_PLAYBOOK.md](SOCIAL_PLAYBOOK.md). It builds on "Layer 3: rewards" and "Pilot
ambassadors" in docs/SOURCING.md and the "Campus ambassadors" idea in docs/IDEAS.md.

All dollar amounts are [$] placeholders to settle with the founder. Nothing here is legal advice.
Counsel questions are at the bottom.

## What creators do
Two jobs, one person.

1. **Make content.** Reels, stories and posts about lines, plans and nights out in [Town]. They
   post on their own accounts and can be featured on ours.
2. **Be early reporters.** On pilot nights they report the line and drink wait from the door
   area at set times (for example every 20 minutes from 10 pm to 1 am, as in SOURCING.md). They
   also run line timers when they are in a line anyway.

Why this matters for data. Early reports seed the numbers while the habit forms. Creators are
trusted reporters. Their reliability starts higher than a new user's but is still learned. The
completed line timers referee everyone, creators included. A creator who reports wrong loses
weight like anyone else. Tell them this plainly. The goal is honest reports, not big numbers.

What creators never do:
- Report from home or from a place they are not at. Location is checked once and only "near or
  not near" is kept, so far away reports count for less.
- Inflate or shrink a line to help a bar, a friend or a story.
- Work for a bar and report on that same bar for pay. Staff already have the door counter.

## Tiers
| Tier | Who | Size | What they do | What they get |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Insiders** | A small paid core | [5 to 8] | Weekly content plus reports on pilot nights | Cash stipend per night, perks, merch, early access, input on the product |
| **Regulars** | Nightlife regulars and org members | [20 to 40] | Report and time lines when out, post when they like | Perks only (no cash), merch, leaderboard |
| **Fans** | Anyone 21+ | Open | Post their own timers and reshare | Repost on our account, shoutout, leaderboard |

Moving up is by behavior, not follower count. A Fan who posts a good timer Reel can become a
Regular. A Regular with steady accurate reports and good content can become an Insider.

"drinks on us". This follows SOURCING.md and BUSINESS.md (tied house and inducement rules).

Pay for the work (content and reports), not for the result (installs or counts). Do not pay per
install in a way that tempts fake signups. Reports are paid by the night and not by the number of
taps.

Note on leaderboards: points reward near-bar reports and timers. Do not publish names, times or
places that show where a person was. Show nickname and points only.

## Recruiting plan
Start with people who already know the nights. Ask for a few from each group, not a crowd from
one.

| Where | How | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Greek life | Contact the social chair or the president of [N] houses. Use the campus org pitch in COPY.md | 1 or 2 Regulars each. Never recruit as a house or tie it to rush. Keep it individual. No pledge or hazing links |
| Clubs and teams | Rec and club sports, a cappella, film, business and marketing clubs | A few 21+ members. A short in person pitch at a meeting |
| Campus media | Student paper, radio, TikTok and film clubs | An Insider or two who already make content |
| Nightlife regulars | People bar staff already know. Ask the pilot bar for names | Regulars who go out Thursday to Saturday anyway |
| Existing beta testers | Close Friends list | Top reporters become Regulars |
| Student employees | Door and bar staff are their own program (door counter). Do not recruit them as creators for the same bar | N/A |

Process:
1. Short form: name, handle, age check ("I am 21 or older"), why, [N] sample posts. No date of
   birth is collected. A 21+ self attestation is the standard, like the app.
2. 10 minute call with the founder or the social lead.
3. Creator brief and agreement signed (counsel template) before any pay or perk.
4. First night with a buddy. Short training on how to report.

Who not to recruit: anyone under 21, anyone whose content is mostly drinking, anyone who cannot
sign a contractor agreement, anyone working for a competing line skip or nightlife app.

## Content rules that protect the brand and people
These are non-negotiable. A broken rule ends the post and, for a repeat, the program.

1. **21+ only, on camera and in the audience.** Every person who appears is 21 or older and has
   said they are OK being filmed. Our captions say 21+. If we cannot be sure of someone's age,
   they are not in the shot.
2. **No excessive drinking.** No drinking games, no chugging, no shots, no "how many did you
   have", no visible intoxication, no drunk driving. No video of someone who is clearly drunk.
3. **No filming people without consent.** Ask first, get a yes on camera or in writing. No
   crowds with identifiable faces. When in doubt, frame the phone screen only, shoot from behind
   or blur.
4. **Bar permission before filming inside.** Ask the manager. Door staff on shift only with their
   and the bar's OK.
5. **No real bar names or logos without Crowded's written OK.** The bar must also say yes, in
   writing. The default in every example and demo is a FAKE bar name.
6. **No university logos, names in trademark styling or marks.** Say "campus" or "[Town]".
7. **Meta rules.** Follow Instagram and Meta's branded content and alcohol policies. Use the
   Paid partnership label. Age-gate alcohol related content to 21+ where the platform allows
   it. Do not promote alcohol, and do not tag alcohol brands. Check Meta's current rules
   before each term.
8. **No live locations of individuals.** No "I'm at [bar] right now" with a person's name, tag or
   location sticker. No geotags on a bar unless it is a partner and said yes. Post a night after
   it happens. No tagging friends at locations. Bar level info ("line is 25 min") is fine.
9. **No private information.** No screenshots of private chats, IDs, phone numbers or names of
   people who did not agree.
10. **Safety first.** Never film while driving or crossing the street. Never ask a person to stay
    out or drink for content. Offer a safe ride link or walking tip when it fits.
11. **No invented numbers.** Say only what the app shows, with its basis. "No recent data" is a
    fine thing to say.
12. **Disclosure on every post made under the program** (see the brief).

## Tracking
Goal: know which creators bring installs and beta signups, with the least data possible.

Approach:
- **A personal invite link or code per creator.** One short code each, for example `INS-[ab12]`.
  Not tied to a name in public. The creator's code goes in their bio link and story sticker.
- **Count by code, not by person.** We store how many installs or beta signups used each code.
  We do not store who clicked, where they were, or what else they did. Aggregate counts only.
- **No tracking pixels.** No Meta pixel, no third party SDK, no ad ID, no fingerprinting, no
  device level matching. This matches the "no location trail" and no raw data rules in CLAUDE.md.
- **Link clicks.** Use a privacy respecting link counter that does not set cookies, or a simple
  redirect on our own site that increments a counter per code and keeps no logs of IPs. Tool:
  [TBD, check that it keeps no personal data].
- **Sheet.** Creator, code, week, clicks, signups, reports that night. Enough to decide who to
  keep.
- **Report with caution.** A code count is a rough signal. Many people join by word of mouth with
  no code. Do not judge a creator on installs alone. Use reports per night and content quality
  too.
- **Do not reward per install.** See compensation above.

**To build (not in the app yet).** The app does not support referral or invite codes today. Until
it does, creators use a link-only method:
1. A separate TestFlight or landing link per creator, counted at the link tool. Clicks only, not
   installs.
2. At signup, an optional "How did you hear about us?" field with a code list. Aggregate only.
   Needs a small backend change (a code field and a counter). Review it against
   docs/SECURITY.md and the threat model before building. It must not store anything about the
   person beyond the code.
3. Later, optional in-app "invite code" at onboarding. Same rules. No attribution SDK.

Hand this item to the lead session as a small backlog item. Not in scope for this doc.

## Sample 6 week pilot plan
For [5 to 8] creators. Mostly Insiders, with a few Regulars. Adjust dates to the real beta.

| Week | Focus | Creators do | Team does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recruit and train | Sign the brief and agreement. 45 minute training on reporting and content rules. Post one intro story | Recruit, contracts (counsel), set up the link tool, create codes, shared sheet |
| 2 | First nights | Report Thursday to Saturday at [10, 10:20, ... 1 am] in pairs. 2 stories and 1 Reel | Check data freshness each night. Fix confusing screens. Quick call Sunday |
| 3 | Content ramps | 2 Reels and 3 stories each. Try ideas 4, 7 and 21 from SOCIAL_PLAYBOOK.md | Review drafts in 24 hours. Pick the best for our account. Pay for week 1 and 2 |
| 4 | First bar live | Cover the first partner bar's nights. Staff POV (idea 17) only with the bar's OK | Feature creator posts. Compare creator reports against staff counts and timers |
| 5 | The big weekend | All hands Thursday to Saturday. Relay stories every [60] minutes. Fan challenge (idea 7) | Weekend recap. Thank everyone. Check disclosure on every post |
| 6 | Review and next | Feedback call. Pick who continues as Insider or moves to Regular | Metrics review. Reliability check. Decide next 6 weeks and budget |

Success measures (set before week 1):
- [N] percent of peak 15 minute intervals have recent data on pilot nights (target 80 percent or
  more, see SOURCING.md).
- Creator report accuracy against completed timers. Reliability scores rising.
- Signups per creator code. A rough signal only.
- Reports per user per night among creators and the people they brought.
- Zero content rule breaks. Zero complaints from a bar.
- Cost per night of the program $[ ].

Stop rule: if a creator breaks the safety rules once, we talk. A second time ends the program for
that person. If a bar objects, take the content down right away.

Budget sketch (placeholders): [N] Insiders x $[ ] per night x [N] nights x 6 weeks, plus content
fees $[ ], merch $[ ], tooling $[ ]. Total $[ ]. Compare with the data value and the cost of
stipends for door staff.

## Legal questions to add for counsel
These extend docs/LEGAL_QUESTIONS.md. That file is not edited here. The lead can merge these in.
Related items already there: 8 (paying ambassadors, FTC disclosures), 10 (inducements and tied
house rules), 18 (raffles and giveaways), 19 (bar names, logos and photos), 20 (university
names).

1. **Contracts.** A short creator agreement template. Term, deliverables, pay, payment timing,
   content ownership and license, take-down rights, confidentiality, no exclusivity, termination
   without cause, a liability cap, and a clause that nobody is expected to drink. Do we need
   a separate release for people who appear on camera?
2. **Contractor status.** Are Insiders independent contractors (1099)? What counts as control
   when we set night schedules and report times? Does a pilot stipend or free cover count as
   pay? State and local rules, and tax forms. What about students who are on work study or
   international students on visas who may not be allowed to earn this pay?
3. **Paying perks and cash with FTC rules.** Does a free cover or a line skip count as a material
   connection that needs disclosure? Is our disclosure wording in the brief enough? Who is
   responsible for a creator's missed disclosure, and what must we monitor?
4. **Giveaway and raffle rules.** Weekly drawings and "post to win". No purchase necessary,
   official rules page, state registration or bond thresholds, age limits (21+), and Instagram's
   promotion rules. Are leaderboard prizes a game of skill or a sweepstake?
5. **Alcohol advertising angle.** Do posts by creators, even about lines at a bar, count as
   alcohol advertising under Pennsylvania or federal rules? Does a post about a bar's cover or
   deal count? Meta's age gate and audience rules. Must content that mentions a bar avoid any
   drink image or name? Is a bar's written permission enough? Do bar crawl or "go out tonight"
   posts create dram shop or social host exposure for us or for creators?
6. **Rewards from bars.** Can partner bars give free cover or a line skip to creators as
   rewards without breaking inducement rules? Do they need to be offered to the public as well?
7. **Minors and consent.** Is on-camera consent in writing needed for everyone who appears?
   What do we do if a minor appears in the background by accident? Takedown process.
8. **University rules.** Do the school or its student org rules limit recruiting through Greek
   life, clubs or campus media, or paid promotion of nightlife? Do we need school approval to
   use campus org names?
9. **Reporter data and privacy.** Creators' reports are part of the estimate. Do we need a
   privacy notice or consent for creators' accounts and codes? Does the personal invite code
   count as personal information under any state law? Is the tracking method described above
   (code counts only, no pixel) enough?
10. **Liability and safety.** Are we exposed if a creator is hurt or harmed on a pilot night?
    Should we have insurance, a safety clause, or a "do not drive, do not drink" training sign
    off?
11. **Employment risk.** Can a bar employee also be a paid creator or reporter? What about
    conflict with their employer?
12. **Content ownership and brand.** License terms for our reposts and ads. Can we run a
    creator's post as a paid ad (whitelisting)? What about music and trademarks in their videos?
