# Business model options (draft for the owner's pricing discussion)

All numbers below are placeholders for discussion, not research. Validate them with the pilot bars.

## Principles
1. **The core stays free for students.** Live waits and crowd levels are the reason students open
   the app and the reason they report. A paywall on the core number kills both.
2. **The number is never for sale.** No payment ever changes a wait, crowd level or verdict.
   Promotions are labeled "Promoted". This trust is the product's moat.
3. **Bars pay for results they can see.** Charge in ways a bar manager can tie to money: more
   students through the door, better staffing, faster door.

## Revenue options
| Option | Who pays | How | Pros | Cons and risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Bar subscription ("Crowded for Bars") | Bar | Free door counter. Pro tier with night reports, peak and capacity trends, staffing hints, comparisons with the town average, promotions | Predictable revenue. Easy to explain | Bars resist monthly fees until the value is proven |
| B. Promotions, pay per result | Bar | Bar posts an offer (no cover before 10, skip the line 9 to 10pm). Pays a small fee per redemption, which staff confirm in the door counter | Bars pay only when students show up. Easiest first sale | Needs redemption tracking. Keep offers non-alcohol (cover, entry, food) |
| C. Passes or cover sold in the app | Student, with a share to Crowded | Crowded sells the line skip pass or the cover. Takes a percentage, or charges the student a small booking fee | Biggest upside. Faster door for bars | Payments, refunds, chargebacks and local rules. The pass verdict becomes a conflict of interest, so it must stay honest and disclosed. Apple allows outside payment for services used in the real world, which needs confirming |
| D. Student premium | Student | Alerts ("line under 10 min at your favorite"), trends, best time to go | Simple | Students pay little. It may split the user base. Later, if at all |
| E. Partnerships | Partners | Rideshare referrals, late night food, campus safe ride programs | Fits the night out | Alcohol brand sponsorship is heavily regulated and an App Store risk. Avoid early |
| F. Aggregate insights | Downtown association, city, campus safety | Anonymous crowd trends for planning and safety | Mission aligned | Small market. Aggregates only, never individuals |

**On a percentage of the cover charge:** this only works if Crowded processes the payment
(option C). Taking a cut of cash collected at the door can't be tracked or enforced. Until
then, use option B, a flat fee per redemption.

## Three kinds of bars, three offers
Real bar names and prices stay out of this public repo. The owner has examples of each type.

| Type | What they look like | What they want | What we sell them | How we charge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The line bar** | Packed every night, cover from early evening, waits that can pass an hour. Unhappy with the current line skip provider | Turn the line into revenue with less friction and a smaller cut | Cover and line skip sold in the app, a faster door, the free counter | A per transaction fee clearly below the incumbent's cut (percentage or a flat student booking fee). Payments needed (option C) |
| **The fast busy bar** | Packed, but the line moves. Cover only some nights | Prove the line is short, staff the door right, plan nights | Analytics (peak times, door throughput, capacity, trends), and a public "short wait even when packed" record built from our data | Monthly subscription (option A) |
| **The hidden gem** | Rarely packed except bar crawls, great deals few people know about, weak location or reputation | Get students through the door | Specials and happy hours shown to students, featured "Deals tonight", events and crawls | Pay per result (option B), or a small monthly listing fee |

The student side benefits from all three: specials, happy hours, cover tonight and events make
the app useful even when lines are short, and give bars a reason to keep their page current.
Showing a bar's own posted specials is information, not an inducement, but Pennsylvania has
specific happy hour and drink pricing rules. Have the lawyers confirm what we can display.

## Recommended path
1. **Pilot, about 3 months: free for bars.** Pay a small stipend to door counters or ambassadors so
   the data is dense. Goal: prove accuracy, freshness and repeat student use.
2. **After proof: founding partner pricing.** A low monthly price for the first bars, locked for a
   year, plus pay per redemption promotions (A + B).
3. **With 8 or more bars in town: passes and cover in the app** (C), plus a Pro analytics tier.
4. Only then, consider student premium (D).

## Questions to settle with lawyers and the pilot bars
- Promotions tied to bars: "tied house" and inducement rules in your state. Keep rewards to cover,
  entry and food, not drinks.
- Selling passes and cover: money transmission, refunds, sales tax, and terms for the bar.
- Disclosure wording when a bar pays for a promotion.
- What a bar would actually pay for first: the door counter and night report, or student traffic.
